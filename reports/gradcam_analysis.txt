CONCLUSIONS
===========

- Did the model focus on meaningful regions?
  Yes. For standard in-distribution images (forest, river, residential), Grad-CAM confirms that the network systematically tracks correct spatial indicators, such as canopy textures for forests, linear flow lines for rivers, and geometric boundaries for residential sectors.

- Did suspicious predictions appear?
  Yes, primarily during out-of-distribution (OOD) tests with noise or unknown classes. The model forces false predictions on random artifacts with moderate confidence while exhibiting scattered heatmaps, demonstrating that closed-world models will silently hallucinate known labels on alien data.

- Can explainability guarantee correctness?
  No. Explainability (XAI) does not fix or guarantee the mathematical correctness of a model's prediction; it merely provides transparency into *why* a decision was reached. A model can confidently make a wrong decision based on a flawed pixel correlation, and Grad-CAM simply unmasks that specific flaw.

- Why is XAI important in operational systems?
  In mission-critical Earth Observation frameworks, XAI acts as a defensive engineering tool. It allows human operators to audit model reasoning, discover silent data corruption, verify robustness against background shortcuts, and safely deploy automated pipelines by understanding exactly when a model's visual logic is failing.
