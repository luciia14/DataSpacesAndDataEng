1. Did fine-tuning improve accuracy?
   Yes, fine-tuning typically improves accuracy (often reaching 100% or very close to it). 
   By unfreezing 'layer4', the model can adapt its high-level feature detectors specifically 
   to satellite imagery textures, which differ significantly from ImageNet's everyday objects.

2. Did training become slower?
   Yes. In the frozen version, we only updated ~1,500 parameters (the 'fc' layer). 
   By unfreezing 'layer4', we are now updating over 8 million parameters. This increases 
   the computational load per epoch and the time spent on backpropagation.

3. Did the confusion matrix change?
   Usually, it shows fewer misclassifications between visually similar classes (like 
   'River' vs 'Forest'). The 'diagonal' of the matrix becomes stronger as the 
   model gains more confidence in its predictions.

4. Did any class improve more than others?
   Classes with complex textures or subtle differences (like 'River' and 'Forest' in 
   satellite view) tend to show the most improvement because the model can now 
   refine its filters to distinguish those specific green/blue patterns.

5. Did any class become worse?
   In very small datasets, sometimes performance can slightly drop due to 'overfitting'. 
   If 'layer4' adapts too much to the training photos, it might lose the ability 
   to generalize to new images, though this is rare with pre-trained models.

6. Is fine-tuning worth the additional computational cost?
   Yes, provided you have a GPU or a reasonably fast CPU. The jump in accuracy and 
   the ability to handle specific data domains (like remote sensing) usually 
   outweighs the extra seconds of training time.

7. Would you use the frozen model or the fine-tuned model in a small-data problem?
   For a 'small-data' problem, I would prefer the FROZEN model or only fine-tuning 
   the very last layer. Fine-tuning too many layers (like 'layer4') on a tiny 
   dataset increases the risk of overfitting, where the model memorizes the data 
   instead of learning it.
